# Toku vs Bitwage (Paystand): A 2026 Stablecoin Payroll Buyer's Guide

In November 2025, Paystand acquired Bitwage. The product still exists. The website still runs. But the buying decision changed. Bitwage is now a feature inside an accounts-payable platform, not a standalone crypto payroll specialist. If you were evaluating these two platforms a year ago, the question was simple: which one fits a crypto-native team? In 2026, the question is sharper. Buyers comparing Toku and Bitwage are really comparing two different products serving two different operational problems.

This guide is for finance, compensation, and people leads at crypto-native and globally distributed teams. We compare Toku and Bitwage (Paystand) on the five decisions that actually drive vendor choice in this category: stablecoin payroll mechanics, token compensation handling, employer of record (EOR) coverage, regulatory framework breadth, and what each platform looks like in production.

## TL;DR

| Question | Toku | Bitwage (Paystand) |
|---|---|---|
| Stablecoin payroll for W-2 employees | ✅ Yes, with US compliance | ✅ Yes, US-domiciled employers |
| Token grants + vesting administration | ✅ Built-in | ❌ Not in scope |
| Employer of Record (multi-jurisdiction) | ✅ Yes, with local entities | ❌ Routes payments, not employs |
| 1099 + W-2 + IR35 + DAC7 filings | ✅ All four | ❌ US filings only |
| Best for | Crypto-native teams hiring globally with token compensation | Freelancer-heavy workflows + accounts-payable integration via Paystand |

If your team needs employment compliance in multiple jurisdictions and you're paying any portion of comp in tokens or stablecoins, the comparison resolves in Toku's direction. If your team is US-centric, paying contractors in stablecoins, and integrating with broader accounts-payable workflows, Bitwage (now Paystand) is the closer fit.

## What Paystand acquiring Bitwage actually changed

Bitwage spent eleven years building crypto payroll for individual freelancers and small employers. The product processed $400M+ across that period and built integrations into ADP and Gusto. The November 2025 acquisition folded it into a $20B accounts-payable platform.

Three things shifted for buyers:

1. **Bitwage's roadmap is now Paystand's roadmap.** Standalone crypto-payroll product development continues, but priorities are set by an AP-platform parent. New features will compound with Paystand's existing B2B payments stack rather than the crypto-payroll ecosystem.
2. **The buyer profile narrowed.** Paystand's customer base is mid-market US businesses running B2B payments. Bitwage's natural place inside that stack is "the crypto rail." Strong for that use case. Less natural for crypto-native companies whose primary problem is global compensation, not US AP automation.
3. **Customer support and continuity are now Paystand's responsibility.** Long-tenured Bitwage customers report the same product. Buyers evaluating today should ask what the product looks like 18 months out.

This isn't a criticism of the acquisition. Paystand is a credible operator and the product hasn't degraded. It's a category shift. Bitwage was a crypto payroll specialist; Bitwage-inside-Paystand is a crypto payment rail inside an AP platform. Different products, different fit.

## How they actually pay your team

Both platforms can send stablecoin payments to employees and contractors. That's the surface match. The mechanics diverge underneath.

**Bitwage (Paystand)** routes payments. The employer funds Bitwage; Bitwage converts (if needed) and sends stablecoin (USDC, USDT) or local-currency payouts to recipient wallets or bank accounts. Bitwage supports W-2 issuance for US-domiciled employers and integrates with ADP and Gusto for fiat payroll runs.

**Toku** runs payroll. Toku acts as the employer or co-employer in 100+ jurisdictions, handles local tax withholding, files the relevant compliance forms (W-2 in the US, IR35 in the UK, DAC7 in the EU, AB5 in California, equivalents elsewhere), and pays employees in stablecoins, local currency, or a hybrid split. The crypto rail is one of several output formats. The platform underneath is global payroll infrastructure.

For a US-only employer paying contractors in stablecoins, this distinction doesn't matter much. For a crypto-native team with engineers in Brazil, designers in Portugal, and a finance lead in Singapore, it's the entire decision.

## Token compensation: where the comparison stops

Token grants, vesting schedules, 83(b) elections, FMV reporting, and the tax filings that follow are outside Bitwage's product scope and have not been a priority post-acquisition. Crypto-native teams that issue any portion of compensation in their own token typically maintain a separate vendor or workflow for grant administration.

Toku handles token grants as a first-class product: vesting tracking, FMV calculations at grant and at each vest event, jurisdiction-specific tax treatment, and the parallel filings that follow (US 83(b), UK CSOP/EMI, EU equivalents). For a Series A crypto-native company issuing tokens to a global team, this is the layer that's usually missing from generic payroll stacks.

If your compensation is 100% stablecoin or fiat, this section is moot. If any portion of comp is in tokens (including future-stage token grants on a vesting cliff), Toku and Bitwage are answering different questions.

## Employer of Record: who actually employs your team

Bitwage doesn't employ workers. It moves payments. For US-domiciled employers, that's enough. The employer is you, the IRS forms come from you, and Bitwage handles the rail.

Toku acts as Employer of Record in 100+ jurisdictions through local legal entities. That means Toku is the legal employer of your team member in their country; you're the operational employer who manages the work. The practical difference shows up the moment you hire someone in a country where you don't have an entity:

- A US company hiring an engineer in Brazil through Toku: Toku employs them under a Brazilian entity, runs Brazilian payroll, handles INSS contributions, issues local pay slips, pays in BRL or USDC depending on local rules.
- A US company hiring the same engineer through Bitwage: the engineer is a contractor of your US entity. You're responsible for any permanent-establishment risk, the engineer is responsible for their own local tax compliance, and the relationship is structurally a contractor relationship regardless of how the work looks.

For one or two early hires, the contractor path is fine. At the point where headcount, hours worked, or work product start to look employer-like under local law, the contractor framing creates risk that Bitwage's product surface does not address.

| Framework | Toku | Bitwage (Paystand) |

| UK IR35 | ✅ | ❌ |
| EU DAC7 | ✅ | ❌ |

| India PMLA / AML | ✅ | ❌ |

This isn't a criticism of Bitwage. The product was never built for multi-jurisdiction employment compliance. It was built for crypto payroll on top of existing employer compliance. Buyers should match the product to their actual regulatory exposure.

## Who should pick what

|---|---|
| US-only, paying contractors in stablecoins, want ADP/Gusto integration | Bitwage (Paystand) |
| US-only, paying W-2 employees in stablecoins, no global hiring planned | Either (depends on whether you also need token compensation) |
| Global team, paying in stablecoins, no token compensation | Toku |

The decision tree compresses to two questions: How many jurisdictions do you employ in, and is any portion of comp in tokens? If both answers are "one" and "no," Bitwage is the lighter integration. If either answer is anything else, the comparison closes in Toku's direction.

## What to ask before signing either contract

Same questions either way:

1. **Where are the legal employer relationships actually structured?** "Crypto payroll" can mean payment routing, EOR, or a contractor middleware. The contract should say which.
2. **Who files the tax forms in each jurisdiction?** If you pay through a vendor but the IRS forms come from you, you're carrying the compliance burden, not them.
3. **What happens if a worker challenges their classification in 18 months?** Both platforms should have a documented answer.
4. **For Bitwage specifically: what's the product roadmap commitment post-acquisition?** Paystand has been a credible operator, but a category shift this large warrants asking how the product is resourced 12-24 months out.
5. **For Toku specifically: what's the per-jurisdiction implementation timeline?** "EOR in 100+ countries" is a capability claim; the relevant question is how fast a specific country goes live for your team.

The vendors that answer these questions cleanly are the ones worth shortlisting.

## Verdict

Bitwage (Paystand) is a crypto payroll rail inside a broader AP platform. Strong fit for US-centric teams running contractor-heavy or freelancer-heavy workflows that already use or want Paystand's accounts payable stack. The product is stable, the operator is credible, and the eleven years of crypto payroll experience didn't disappear in the acquisition.

Toku is global payroll infrastructure with crypto compensation built in. Built for crypto-native and globally distributed teams that need W-2, 1099, IR35, and DAC7 coverage alongside token grant administration as primary product surface, not as adjacencies.

The two products overlap on the surface (stablecoin payments) and diverge underneath (who employs your team, who files the compliance forms, and whether token compensation is in scope at all). Match the product to the problem.
