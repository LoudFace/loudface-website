The starting point: 0.13%

In April 2026 LoudFace was named in 3 of 2,306 monitored AI answers to non-branded buyer prompts in our category, 0.13% of them. For a buyer asking ChatGPT, Perplexity, or Google AI Overviews "who's the best B2B SaaS organic growth agency," we did not exist.

We had one structural problem that no amount of content volume fixes on its own: a Domain Rating around 31. In classic Google search, authority gates everything, and at DR 31 you wait years to outrank incumbents with a decade of links. So we did not bet on Google authority. We bet on the channel where page structure beats domain authority, which is AI answers, and we ran our own pipeline on our own site to prove the bet.

The method, step by step

1. We ran our client process on ourselves. Every page was grounded in live search and AI-answer data and checked against its sources before it shipped. Running it on our own domain was the test.

2. We treated the top of every page as the citation surface. AI engines lift their answer from the first lines of a page rather than the body. So every cornerstone page opens with a direct answer of roughly 40 to 60 words that an engine can quote whole, uses question-shaped headings, and closes with a structured FAQ. We shipped FAQPage and Article schema on every post, because a page a machine can parse cleanly is a page it can quote.

3. We concentrated instead of narrowing. Our binding constraint was DR 31 gating the citation-to-ranking conversion, plus citation fragmentation: near-duplicate pages splitting the same answer across five URLs. The fix is consolidation: fold near-synonym pages into one canonical entity page so the authority and the citations pool in one place. We did that across the site rather than chasing more topics.

4. We built the pages AI actually cites. In our category the cited surface is decision content: "best organic growth agencies," "best AEO agency for fintech," agency pricing and comparison pages. We built that cluster deliberately, structured each page for extraction, and stamped every one with the year and a visible update date, because the engines reward freshness.

5. We picked targets from real demand rather than a keyword tool. Two signals drove the roadmap. The first was buyer prompts where every competitor scored zero, uncontested questions we could own outright. The second was our own AI-bot 404 logs: when ChatGPT's crawler fetches a URL on our domain that does not exist, the model has inferred a page should be there, which is a content brief that bypasses keyword research entirely.

6. We measured with a real stack and kept the metrics separate. We track share of answer in Peec across a panel of buyer prompts, Google Search Console for clicks and impressions, and our own server logs, which are the highest-fidelity signal because they record what actually arrived rather than what a model estimates. We never conflate the three things most teams blur together: citation speed, visibility (how often you appear at all), and share of voice (how much of the answer is yours). Our headline number is visibility.

Google moved too, more slowly

This growth happened in AI answers first, and that was the plan. Google moved too, more slowly. Our average position in Google Search Console went from 23.8 in April to 10.1 in September (1 to 21), and impressions per day are 2.5 times April's. Clicks lag: at 6.9 a day in September, they are still below the 8.7 to 8.8 a day of September and October 2025.

That gap is what we bet on. Structure, freshness, and specificity decide who gets cited in AI answers far more than raw Domain Rating does. So a domain rated around 31 in April, and 36 on 23 September, can be named in a fifth of its category's non-branded AI answers while its Google clicks are still catching up, because authority still gates the citation-to-ranking conversion in classic search. AI search is where a smaller brand can win now, by being built to be quoted. Google compounds slowly behind it. Update, 29 September 2026: the reading after the prompt cut, and the first prospects it sent us

On non-branded prompts, visibility went from 12.3% in August to 21.5% over 18 to 28 September, and we moved from 10th to 3rd of the 53 tracked brands. That window starts the day after we cut the prompt panel, so it is measured on the 141 non-branded prompts we track after the cut.

The cut removed prompts where we rarely appeared, so it lifts the reading. The number that matters more than the leaderboard position is what it changed on our own pipeline. Prospects tell us on discovery calls that ChatGPT recommended us, and since 20 August our booking form has asked where people heard of us: from then to 22 September, AI assistants were named as often as Google search, on a small number of bookings. Sales calls booked in August were 1.8 times the May to July monthly average. September, to the 22nd, is running below August's pace, so we are not claiming a trend in calls yet.

Limitations, stated as method

The sample is one brand, our own, from 8 April to 28 September 2026, and the percentages climb off a near-zero April base. Our panel covers three engines, ChatGPT, Perplexity, and Google AI Overviews, rather than every AI surface. Visibility is the share of answers that mention us; share of voice, the share of the answer that is ours, is a stricter and lower number.

The prompt panel is not identical across the window. It grew from April to June; on 2 September we added a large batch of new prompts, 88 of which are still tracked, including the four about LoudFace itself; on 17 September we archived 67 prompts, most of them with low or zero visibility for us; and on 20 September we added five more. The panel stands at 150 prompts today. Each month in the tables is measured on the panel as it stood that month, and the readings after 17 September are lifted by the cut.

The sales-call figure counts distinct people booking an intro call or quick chat each month, excluding our own test bookings, hiring interviews and cancellations. Counting every booking event and averaging April to July instead puts August at 2.0 times; the clean count reads 1.8 times. April is left out because booking tracking began on 20 April. And Peec's citation counts are a modeled daily sample rather than server logs, which is why we cross-check against our own logs. None of this changes the direction. It changes how loudly we can claim the size.
